The 10-minute AI tool audit

A practical check on the AI your business already uses.

Download the free checklist (PDF)

Free. No email required. The PDF is the one-page sheet to print and fill in.

AI use has doubled in a year among UK businesses with five or more staff. The government's AI adoption survey, published on 29 October 2026, found that 32% were using at least one AI technology, up from 16% a year earlier. Among firms already using AI, 64% saw no change in their operating costs. (GOV.UK, AI adoption survey 2026, executive summary, 29 October 2026)

In research from Air IT Group, 41% said their organisation bought or introduced an AI tool before agreeing what problem it should solve. (Air IT Group, The AI Decision Gap)

You can't fix that without a list. This audit takes about ten minutes.

How to do it

  1. Write down every AI tool or AI feature your business uses, paid or free, including features inside software you already have.
  2. Next to each one, finish this line: "This saves us [what], measured by [how]."
  3. Check whether the tool stores customer data, and where.
  4. Anything you can't fill in gets one month to prove itself, or it goes.

If you use one free chatbot for drafting and nothing else, you can skip this.

Example only (not a real firm): "This saves us two hours of invoice chasing a week, measured by the days from sending an invoice to getting paid."

Your list

Date of this audit: _______________

Tool or AI feature Paid or free This saves us [what], measured by [how] Stores customer data? Where? Keep, one month, or drop

Then decide

  • Keep it if you can finish the sentence, and you know whether it stores customer data, and where.
  • Give it one month if you think it helps but you have not measured it yet. Write a date and a simple measure.
  • Drop it if nobody uses it, or you cannot name the job it does.

Customer data. Check whether the tool stores customer data, and where. This is a practical prompt, not legal advice. The ICO publishes guidance for small organisations at ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-for-small-organisations.

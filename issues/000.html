Greater Manchester counts its AI firms, plus free help worth asking for

Hello,

Welcome to the first issue. Your five-minute read: what's changing locally, one thing worth trying, and where to find funding and support. Every figure links to its source. If we get something wrong, reply and we'll correct it.

What's changing locally

Greater Manchester has counted its AI firms for the first time. A study commissioned by the Greater Manchester Combined Authority (GMCA) and produced by Perspective Economics found 663 businesses developing or using AI across the city region. More than half (54%) were founded locally, and an estimated 10,890 people work in AI-related roles, the largest AI workforce of any UK region outside London. Under a medium-growth scenario, the study suggests around 1,420 firms and more than 28,000 AI-related jobs by 2035. (GMCA, 19 Oct 2026; full report, PDF)

Why it matters to you: the report says the region's strength is applied AI, meaning AI put to work in existing industries and public services. That's most of us.

Liverpool City Region is looking at shared AI computing. The Combined Authority is working with NVIDIA and K2K to assess whether secure, shared regional computing could help public services, researchers and businesses use AI. The first phase, expected to start in late October, brings in the city region's six councils and looks at demand, data security, skills, energy use and affordability. Any future proposal would go through public procurement and the Combined Authority's governance. (LCRCA, 14 Oct 2026)

A Manchester start-up has won £100,000 for AI that designs metal-capturing proteins. LanthaGen Bio won the first Coller AI Competition at the University of Manchester's Innovation Festival, with a £100,000 equity investment from the Jeremy Coller Foundation. Its AI platform simulates how biomolecules bind metals, so proteins that capture rare earth metals can be designed on screen instead of by trial and error. (Insider Media, 12 Oct 2026)

Also noted: Manchester AI marketing start-up Revnu has raised $3m in a round led by NPIF II, PXN Equity Finance (Prolific North, 14 Oct 2026).

Timely: check your website after the PHP alert

On 21 Oct the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) urged UK organisations to act on a critical security issue in PHP, the programming language behind a large share of websites. It said the issue "may allow attackers to execute commands on any web server running PHP" and that all versions may be affected. (NCSC alert, 21 Oct 2026, updated 23 Oct) PHP powers WordPress, and the BBC reports the flaws were first discovered by AI. (BBC News, 22 Oct 2026)

Do this week: ask whoever built or hosts your site, in writing: "Has our site been updated for the PHP security issue the NCSC flagged on 21 October?" Check plugins and "all-in-one" products too, because the NCSC says some bundle PHP and need their own updates.

One thing worth trying: AI drafts for the enquiries you answer every week

The job: the same questions arrive every week ("Do you cover Bolton?", "What's your lead time?").

The tool: if your business already uses Google Workspace, Google lists a "Gemini AI assistant in Gmail" even on its cheapest Business plan, Business Starter. Google's UK pricing shows Starter at £5.90 per user per month on a one-year commitment, or £7 if you pay monthly, excluding VAT. (Google Workspace UK pricing and Google's billing plan comparison, checked 23 Oct 2026)

Try it this week:

  1. Pick three enquiries you answer all the time.
  2. Write your ideal reply to each one yourself, once.
  3. Ask the assistant to draft new replies in the same style, then edit before you send.

When not to bother:

  • On Microsoft 365? Don't switch just for this.
  • If a reply involves customers' personal details, read the ICO's guidance for small organisations before putting them into any AI tool (ICO).
  • Always read the draft before sending.

Funding and support

All checked against the official pages on 23 Oct 2026. Eligibility rules apply.

For most local businesses

  • GC Business Growth Hub (Greater Manchester). Publicly funded support at no charge. (Official page, last checked 23 Oct 2026)
  • AIMS for SMEs (Manchester Digital, North West). Free. Over eight weeks, a small team looks at one of your manual or repetitive processes to see where AI could help. No technical knowledge needed. (Official page, last checked 23 Oct 2026)
  • ChallengeAI Accelerator (Manchester Metropolitan University). A fully funded four-day programme for Greater Manchester businesses to test and prototype an AI idea. Register interest for the next cohort. Later technical projects need a contribution from you. (Official page, last checked 23 Oct 2026)
  • AI Adoption Readiness (Edge Hill University). A three-part seminar series for senior leaders on AI and leading change. Fully funded for SMEs in the Liverpool City Region and West Lancashire. (Official page, last checked 23 Oct 2026)
  • Help to Grow: Management (University of Liverpool). A 12-week, 90% funded leadership and growth course for SME senior managers. (Official page, last checked 23 Oct 2026)

For manufacturers

  • Made Smarter Adoption: North West. Specialist advice for SME manufacturers, and up to 50% match funding (maximum £20,000) for equipment or specialist advice. (Official page, last checked 23 Oct 2026)

For businesses that build AI

  • BridgeAI Deployment Catalyst (Innovate UK). Open now, closing 8 Dec 2026 at 11:00. It has three strands (operational, decision-critical, and assurance and compliance), each with up to £5 million in total. Projects ask for £100,000 to £200,000. It's for AI firms with a working product and a medium or large partner that will use it, not for generic productivity tools. (Innovation Funding Service, last checked 23 Oct 2026)
  • Efficient, Structured and Controllable AI Systems (Innovate UK). Up to £1.66 million in total for early-stage AI research. Closes 18 Nov 2026 at 11:00. The competition page says: "Our experience from similar competitions suggests that you could have 4% chance of success." Only apply if you're a research-heavy AI firm. (Innovation Funding Service, last checked 23 Oct 2026)

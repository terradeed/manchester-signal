AI answers are replacing clicks: what that means for a local firm

Hello,

This week: why AI answers are replacing clicks for local firms, what the Budget confirmed about local AI money, a Warrington farm that built its own booking system, and one narrow job worth picking this week. Every figure links to its source. If we get something wrong, reply and we'll correct it.

When someone asks an AI for a local firm, the answer comes before the click

On Reddit's Q3 2026 earnings call on 29 Oct, CEO Steve Huffman said Google's AI Mode "does not drive traffic, not for us and not to anybody as far as I know... It used to be a traffic driver. Now it's not." Reddit also said October search referrals were down by more than 1 million a week on average. (The Motley Fool transcript, 30 Oct 2026; also The Verge, 29 Oct 2026)

For a North West owner, the same shift shows up closer to home. When someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI for "an accountant in Bolton" (an example, not a real case), the AI often answers on the spot. What matters is whether it names you, not whether anyone clicks through a list of links.

Three plain steps that help:

  1. Say clearly on your site what you do and which town you serve.
  2. Keep your Google Business Profile and reviews up to date.
  3. Add an FAQ page in plain words that answers the questions customers already ask.

None of that guarantees an AI will name you. It does make it easier for search engines and AI tools to describe you accurately.

Why it matters to you: if your site is thin or your Google profile is out of date, you are harder to name when someone asks an AI who to call locally.

What's changing locally

The Budget confirmed £30m for local AI partnerships. The first pilots are in Yorkshire, not the North West. Manchester gets a new AI cyber centre. Ahead of the Budget, the Treasury announced £30m "to kickstart local AI partnerships with Mayoral Strategic Authorities", aimed at councils, businesses, universities and colleges working on local AI adoption, including support for SMEs and AI skills for workers. (HM Treasury, 24 Oct 2026) The Budget on 28 Oct confirmed the £30m and named the first pilots: West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, and York and North Yorkshire Combined Authorities. (LGA Budget briefing, 29 Oct 2026; Prolific North, 29 Oct 2026) The same package establishes a new AI Cyber Security Centre of Excellence in Manchester to train the next generation of government cyber and AI experts. (HM Treasury, 24 Oct 2026; House of Lords written statement, 26 Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you: the first £30m pilots are in Yorkshire. There is no North West application window yet. Watch whether your combined authority is added later. The Manchester centre is about training government specialists, not a grant you apply for.

Greater Manchester has a new innovation plan, with a service to help firms find support. The 2035 Greater Manchester Innovation Plan, launched on 21 Oct, includes almost £10 million for an Innovation Navigation Service to help businesses reach expertise and funding. It also confirms the £50m Local Innovation Partnerships Fund for 2026 to 2031, with applied AI as one of its focus areas. (GMCA, 21 Oct 2026; the plan, PDF)

Why it matters to you: there's no date yet for when the navigation service opens to firms. We'll tell you when there's a way in.

A Warrington fruit farm built its own ticketing system with AI. Kenyon Hall Farm replaced an expensive third-party ticketing system for its Pick Your Own visits with a platform it built during the University of Salford's six-week BUILD AI programme. The farm says the platform cut its annual software costs by more than 90%, and that it is used by around 10,000 people a week at peak. The savings went into extra seasonal jobs. (Warrington Worldwide, 8 Oct 2026)

Why it matters to you: the job was narrow and well understood: booking slots around fruit availability. That is where building your own tool can pay off. We couldn't find an open BUILD AI intake at the time of writing (the 2026 cohort closed applications on 7 Apr 2026).

One thing worth doing: name one narrow job before you buy anything

The job: picking the one process you'd rebuild first, if AI were going to earn its keep.

Why this week: Kenyon Hall Farm's story works because the problem was narrow and well understood. Most AI spends fail the other way: a vague brief, then a tool that does a bit of everything poorly.

Try it this week:

  1. Write down one job that costs you time or money every week (for example booking slots, repeating the same enquiry reply, or moving the same data between two systems).
  2. Ask one question of yourself, in writing: "Could a simple tool do just this job, and nothing else?"
  3. If you want free help exploring that kind of process, look at AIMS for SMEs (Manchester Digital): over eight weeks, a small team looks at one manual or repetitive process. It is free for North West SMEs. (Official page, last checked 30 Oct 2026)

When not to bother:

  • If you already have a written list of three processes and a named owner for the first one, you're ahead. Stop and do the first one.
  • Don't buy a platform because a supplier demo looked clever. Start from the job.
  • If the process holds customers' personal details, read the ICO's guidance for small organisations before putting them into any AI tool. (ICO)
